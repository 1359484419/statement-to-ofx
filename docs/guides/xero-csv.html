Importing a bank statement CSV into Xero

Xero can import statement lines from a CSV file when there is no bank feed, or to fill a gap in one. The file has to follow Xero's simple layout. Here is what each column means and where imports usually trip.

The layout this converter writes

ColumnContent
*DateTransaction date, in the format you choose (see below).
*AmountSigned number with a dot as decimal separator and no thousands separator: -42.10 for money out, 1500.00 for money in.
PayeeThe description from your statement. Xero's bank rules match on this, so keep it close to what the bank prints.
DescriptionExtra text (memo, original description) when the source has it.
ReferenceTransaction or document number, if any.
Check NumberCheque number, if any.

The asterisks mark the columns that must have a value. The names follow the layout of Xero's statement import template. During import Xero asks you to confirm which column holds what, so if your version labels a column differently, map it there.

Dates: match your organisation's region

Xero reads ambiguous dates such as 03/04/2026 according to the organisation's regional settings. A US organisation reads it as 4 March; a UK, Australian or New Zealand one as 3 April. Pick MM/DD/YYYY or DD/MM/YYYY in the export step to match. After importing, check that the first and last statement lines show the dates you expect before reconciling.

Signs

Money leaving the account is negative. For a credit card account in Xero, a purchase is also negative and a payment to the card is positive. If your card statement prints purchases as positive, tick “Flip all signs” in the converter before exporting.

Avoiding duplicates

A CSV line has no unique ID, so overlapping imports are easy to create. Export one statement period at a time, and use the period shown in the review step to check that it does not overlap your last import.

CSV or OFX?

Xero also imports OFX files. OFX carries the account, the closing balance and a unique ID per line, which helps with duplicate detection. Use the CSV when you want to edit lines in a spreadsheet before importing, or when your bank's description text is better kept in separate Payee and Description columns.

Doing it

  1. Open the converter and drop your bank's CSV, Excel or text PDF statement.
  2. Check the columns and the balance checks, fix any highlighted rows.
  3. Choose the date format that matches your Xero organisation and click Xero bank statement CSV.
  4. In Xero, open the bank account, choose to import a statement, and select the file.
